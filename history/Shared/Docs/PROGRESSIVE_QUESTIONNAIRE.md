# First Try Design: Progressive Questionnaire

Design through conversation, not specifications. The system asks questions people can actually answer, then translates to constraints.

---

## Core Principle

People don't think in square footage. They think in life:
- "I need space for a queen bed with room to walk around"
- "We host Thanksgiving for 15 people"
- "I want to watch the kids while I cook"

The system asks about life, then computes the architecture.

---

## Progressive Revelation

The plan grows as you answer questions:

| After Group | What Exists |
|-------------|-------------|
| 1. Household | Room count, accessibility requirements |
| 2. Daily Life | Room relationships, entry sequence, rough zones |
| 3. Rooms | Room sizes (from furniture/activities) |
| 4. Site | Orientation, window placement, entry location |
| 5. Feel | Openness, ceiling heights, material character |

You can see the design evolving. Each group refines and locks in decisions.

---

## Group 1: Household

**Purpose:** Understand who lives here and basic needs.

**Questions:**

1. Who lives in this home?
   - Adults (number, relationships)
   - Children (number, ages)
   - Others (elderly parent, caregiver, etc.)

2. Any accessibility needs?
   - Mobility aids (wheelchair, walker)
   - Aging in place considerations
   - Sensory needs

3. Pets?
   - Type, size, number
   - Special needs (dog washing, cat spaces, fenced yard)

4. Does anyone work from home?
   - Occasionally (laptop at kitchen table)
   - Regularly (dedicated space needed)
   - Client-facing (separate entrance needed)

5. How long do you plan to live here?
   - Starter home (5 years)
   - Forever home (aging in place)
   - Flexible (might expand family, might downsize)

**System generates:**
- Bedroom count
- Bathroom count (rough)
- Office requirement
- Accessibility level (full, visitability, future-adaptable)
- Flexibility requirements

---

## Group 2: Daily Life

**Purpose:** Understand how the house flows.

**Questions:**

1. Walk me through a typical morning.
   - Where do people wake up, get ready, eat, leave?
   - Bottlenecks? (everyone in one bathroom)
   - Who leaves first? Who's home longest?

2. How do you enter the house most days?
   - Front door
   - Through garage
   - Back/side door
   - Multiple entries for different people

3. When you come home, what happens first?
   - Drop keys, bags, shoes
   - Kids drop backpacks
   - Dog needs to go out
   - Groceries to kitchen

4. Where does the family gather?
   - Formal living room
   - Open kitchen/family room
   - Outdoor space
   - Everyone in their own rooms

5. How often do you cook? For how many?
   - Rarely (microwave, takeout)
   - Daily for family
   - Entertain frequently (dinner parties)
   - Big holiday gatherings (number of guests)

6. Do kids need supervision while you cook?
   - Yes, need sightlines to play area
   - No, kids are older/independent
   - Play area should be visible from multiple spots

7. Where does laundry happen?
   - Near bedrooms (upstairs)
   - Near kitchen (main floor)
   - Basement
   - Doesn't matter

8. Do you need a formal dining room?
   - Yes, separate from everyday eating
   - No, one eating area is fine
   - Kitchen island/bar handles daily, table for guests

**System generates:**
- Entry sequence (mudroom, drop zone)
- Kitchen/living relationship (open, semi-open, separate)
- Supervision sightlines
- Dining configuration
- Laundry location zone
- Public vs. private zone separation

**Plan shows:**
- Bubble diagram with zones
- Entry flow
- Rough adjacencies

## Group 3: Rooms

**Purpose:** Size rooms based on what happens in them, not abstract numbers.

**Approach:** For each room, ask what furniture/activities, not dimensions.

### Bedroom Questions

**Primary Bedroom:**
1. What size bed?
   - Queen
   - King
   - California King

2. How do you access the bed?
   - Both sides (two people)
   - One side is against wall (okay)
   - Need wheelchair clearance

3. What else is in the bedroom?
   - Just the bed and nightstands
   - Dresser
   - Seating area (chair, small couch)
   - Desk/workspace
   - TV
   - Exercise equipment

4. Do you need a walk-in closet?
   - No, reach-in is fine
   - Yes, for one person
   - Yes, for two people (his/hers or shared?)

5. Ensuite bathroom?
   - No, hall bath is fine
   - Yes, basic (toilet, sink, shower)
   - Yes, with tub
   - Yes, with double vanity
   - Spa-like (soaking tub, large shower, etc.)

**Other Bedrooms:**
1. Who uses this room?
   - Child (age?)
   - Guest
   - Flexible (office now, bedroom later)

2. What bed size?
   - Crib/toddler now, twin later
   - Twin
   - Full
   - Queen (guest room)

3. What else?
   - Desk for homework
   - Play area
   - Dresser
   - Closet needs (kid clothes vs. guest storage)

**System translates:**
- Queen + 3ft walk around = 12' x 12' minimum
- King + both sides + dresser + seating = 14' x 16'
- Twin + desk + play area = 10' x 11'
- Walk-in closet for two = 8' x 10'
- Ensuite with double vanity and shower = 8' x 10'

### Living Area Questions

1. What furniture?
   - Sofa size (love seat, 3-seat, sectional)
   - Chairs (how many)
   - Coffee table
   - TV (size, wall mount or console)
   - Fireplace

2. How many people should it seat comfortably?
   - Family only (number)
   - Family + a few guests
   - Large gatherings (10+)

3. What happens here?
   - TV watching
   - Conversation
   - Kids playing on floor
   - Reading
   - Board games

**System translates:**
- Sectional + 2 chairs + play area = 18' x 20'
- Small sofa + 2 chairs, no play area = 14' x 16'

### Kitchen Questions

1. How many cooks at once?
   - One
   - Two (need space to pass)

2. Island or no island?
   - No island
   - Island for prep only
   - Island with seating (how many stools?)

3. What appliances?
   - Standard (fridge, range, dishwasher)
   - Double oven
   - Large fridge / separate freezer
   - Wine fridge
   - Coffee station

4. Pantry?
   - Cabinet pantry (couple shelves)
   - Walk-in pantry
   - No strong preference

5. Eat-in kitchen?
   - No, dining elsewhere
   - Small table (2-4)
   - Island seating covers it

**System translates:**
- 2 cooks + island with 4 stools + walk-in pantry = 14' x 18'
- 1 cook + no island + cabinet pantry = 10' x 12'

### Other Rooms

**Office:**
1. What furniture?
   - Desk + chair
   - Desk + bookshelf + filing
   - Two desks (shared office)
   - Client meeting space

2. Do clients/coworkers visit?
   - No, private work only
   - Occasionally
   - Regularly (need separate entrance?)

**Mudroom/Entry:**
1. How many people gear up at once?
2. What needs storage? (coats, shoes, bags, sports equipment)
3. Dog washing station?

**Laundry:**
1. Side by side or stacked?
2. Folding space needed?
3. Hang dry space?
4. Sink?

**Garage:**
1. How many cars?
2. What else? (workshop, storage, bikes, lawn equipment)
3. Access to house (direct to kitchen? mudroom?)

**System generates:**
- All room sizes (computed from furniture + clearances)
- Special features per room

**Plan shows:**
- Rooms sized and shaped
- Still moveable, but proportions locked

## Group 4: Site

**Purpose:** Lock orientation and placement on the lot.

**Questions:**

1. Which way does the lot face?
   - Front of lot faces: N / S / E / W
   - Or: "I don't know, here's the address"

2. Where's the street?
   - Front
   - Corner lot (two streets)
   - Flag lot (long driveway)

3. What do you see from each direction?
   - North: neighbor's house, nice yard, trees, parking lot, mountain, etc.
   - South: ...
   - East: ...
   - West: ...

4. Any views to capture?
   - "Mountain view to the north"
   - "Backyard faces a park"
   - "Nothing special"

5. Any views to block?
   - "Neighbor's ugly shed to the west"
   - "Busy street to the south"
   - "Nothing to hide from"

6. Where should the sun be?
   - Morning light in bedrooms? (east windows)
   - Evening light in living room? (west windows)
   - Avoid afternoon heat? (minimize west glass)
   - Maximize passive solar? (south glass)

7. Privacy concerns?
   - Street is busy, want privacy in front
   - Neighbors are close on one side
   - Backyard is private
   - Want bedroom away from street noise

8. Outdoor spaces?
   - Front porch (casual, social)
   - Back patio (private, entertaining)
   - Side yard (utility, dog run)
   - Balcony/deck
   - Garden

9. Where do cars go?
   - Front-facing garage
   - Side-entry garage
   - Detached garage
   - Driveway only (no garage)

10. Any site features to preserve or avoid?
    - Big tree to keep
    - Slope/drainage issues
    - Existing structure
    - Utility easement

**System generates:**
- Building orientation on lot
- Window directions per room
- Entry and garage placement
- Outdoor space locations
- Setback compliance check

**Plan shows:**
- Building placed on site
- Orientation locked
- Windows indicated (direction, not detailed)

---

## Group 5: Feel

**Purpose:** Character, openness, and material palette.

**Questions:**

1. Open or defined?
   - Open: kitchen/living/dining flow together
   - Semi-open: some separation but connected
   - Defined: distinct rooms with doors/walls

2. Ceiling height?
   - Standard (8'-9') feels cozy
   - Tall (10'+) feels open
   - Vaulted in main spaces
   - No preference

3. How much natural light?
   - Lots of windows, bright as possible
   - Balanced, not too bright
   - Cozy, smaller windows okay

4. Connection to outdoors?
   - Big sliding doors, indoor-outdoor living
   - Windows are enough
   - Private, minimal glass

5. Formal or casual?
   - Formal (defined entry, separate dining, etc.)
   - Casual (drop your stuff anywhere, open hang)
   - Mix (formal for guests, casual daily areas)

6. Minimal or warm?
   - Minimal: clean lines, less stuff, calm
   - Warm: texture, wood, collected feel
   - Mix

7. Any style keywords?
   - Modern, farmhouse, craftsman, mid-century, traditional, etc.
   - Or: "I don't know, just make it nice"

8. Anything to avoid?
   - "Hate white kitchens"
   - "No carpet"
   - "Nothing too trendy"

**System generates:**
- Wall openness (where walls are removed)
- Ceiling heights per room
- Window sizes (relative)
- Material palette hints
- Style parameters for future detailing

**Plan shows:**
- Final floor plan
- Ceiling heights indicated
- Wall openings shown
- Window sizes roughed in

## Translation Examples

| Human Input | System Constraint |
|-------------|-------------------|
| "Queen bed, walk around both sides" | Bedroom min 12' x 12' |
| "King bed, one side against wall" | Bedroom min 10' x 13' |
| "Sectional sofa, 6 people comfortable" | Living min 16' x 18' |
| "Island with 4 stools" | Kitchen min 12' x 14' |
| "Thanksgiving for 15" | Dining min 14' x 16' or open to living |
| "Watch kids while cooking" | Kitchen-living sightline required |
| "Morning light in bedroom" | Primary bedroom window faces east |
| "Work from home, video calls" | Office has door, window for background |
| "Elderly parent visits" | Main floor bedroom + bath option |
| "Hate being seen from street" | Living room not at front, or privacy screen |

## Conversation Flow

The system asks these as a conversation, not a form.

**Example:**

> "Let's start with who lives here. How many adults and kids?"

*Two adults, two kids — 8 and 5.*

> "Got it. Any pets?"

*One dog, medium sized.*

> "Does anyone work from home?"

*I do, about 3 days a week. Video calls.*

> "Do clients ever come to your home office?"

*No, just me.*

> "Okay. Anyone with mobility needs, or planning to age in place here?"

*My mom might move in eventually. She uses a walker.*

> "Good to know. Let me make sure we have main-floor living options for her."

**After Group 1:**
> "Based on what you've told me: 4 bedrooms (master, 2 kids, guest/mom), 2.5 baths minimum, home office, and we'll plan for accessibility on the main floor. Here's a rough program..."

---

## Error Handling

If answers conflict, the system asks:

> "You said you want an open plan but also a formal dining room. Do you want:
> - Dining open to living, but defined by ceiling or flooring change
> - Dining as a separate room with walls
> - Dining that can be closed off (sliding doors, etc.)"

If constraints can't be met:

> "With a 1,400 sf limit and everything you've described, something has to give. Your priorities are natural light and open plan. Would you rather:
> - Reduce one bedroom size
> - Shrink the office
> - Lose the formal entry"

---

## Output After All Groups

Once all groups are answered:

1. **Complete floor plan** — optimized to constraints
2. **Decision summary** — what drove each major choice
3. **Assumptions** — anything the system inferred
4. **Tradeoffs made** — what was sacrificed, why

If you don't like it, review the summary. The answer to "why is the bedroom here?" is in your answers.

---

## The Philosophy Restated

You don't need to know architecture. You need to know your life.

The system's job is to ask the right questions, translate your answers into constraints, and find the optimal solution.

If the solution is wrong, the questions were answered wrong — or incompletely. Go back, clarify, regenerate.

One try. But you can take your time with the questions.
