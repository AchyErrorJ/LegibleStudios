# Client Onboarding Process

Turn intake into design. Capture everything upfront, show up with answers.

---

## The Problem with Traditional Onboarding

**Meeting 1:** Discovery
- Chat for an hour
- Take scattered notes
- "We'll get back to you with some ideas"

**Back at the office:**
- Try to remember what they said
- Fill in gaps with assumptions
- Design based on incomplete information

**Meeting 2:** Present concepts
- "That's not what we meant"
- "Oh we forgot to mention..."
- "We assumed you knew we wanted..."

**Repeat** until something sticks or everyone's exhausted.

---

## The New Process

**Before Meeting 1:** Client completes questionnaire
- Online, self-paced
- Takes 30-60 minutes
- All the questions they need to answer anyway

**Meeting 1:** Review generated plan
- Show up with a design already done
- Based entirely on their answers
- Meeting is reaction and refinement, not discovery

**After Meeting 1:** Refine inputs, regenerate
- Adjust answers based on discussion
- System regenerates
- Client sees direct link between input and output

**Meeting 2:** Final review or done
- Often done in 2 meetings instead of 5

---

## Workflow Overview

```
Client Inquiry
      ↓
Send Questionnaire Link
      ↓
Client Completes (self-paced, 30-60 min)
      ↓
System Generates Initial Plan
      ↓
Designer Reviews (flag issues, prepare questions)
      ↓
Meeting 1: Review Together
      ↓
Refine Inputs Based on Discussion
      ↓
Regenerate Plan
      ↓
Meeting 2: Confirm or Iterate
      ↓
Design Complete → Move to Documentation
```

## Phase 1: Initial Contact

### Client Reaches Out

When a potential client contacts you:

1. **Quick qualification call (10-15 min)**
   - Project type (new construction, renovation, addition)
   - Timeline expectations
   - Budget ballpark (are we in the same universe?)
   - Site status (do they own it, under contract, looking)

2. **If qualified, send questionnaire link**
   - Explain the process
   - "This replaces our usual 2-hour discovery meeting"
   - "Fill it out before we meet, and I'll have a draft plan ready"

3. **Set expectations**
   - Takes 30-60 minutes to complete
   - Can save and return
   - Be honest — the plan is only as good as the answers
   - Schedule Meeting 1 for after they complete it

### Email Template: Sending Questionnaire

```
Subject: Your Design Questionnaire — [Project Name]

Hi [Client],

Great talking with you! I'm excited about your project.

Before we meet, I'd like you to complete our design questionnaire. 
It takes about 30-60 minutes and covers:

- How you live and what spaces you need
- Site information (for new construction) or existing conditions (for renovation)
- Priorities and tradeoffs
- Style and feel

Here's the link: [questionnaire URL]

You can save your progress and come back anytime.

Once you've completed it, our system generates an initial design based 
entirely on your answers. When we meet, we'll already have something 
to look at and react to — instead of starting from scratch.

Our meeting is scheduled for [date/time]. Please complete the 
questionnaire at least 2 days before so I have time to review.

Questions? Just reply to this email.

[Signature]
```

## Phase 2: Questionnaire Completion

### What the Client Sees

**Welcome screen:**
- Explain the process
- Set time expectation (30-60 min)
- "Your answers directly shape the design"

**Progress indicator:**
- Shows which group they're in
- Shows completion percentage
- "Save & Continue Later" always visible

**Question groups (new construction):**
1. Household (10 min)
2. Daily Life (10 min)
3. Room by Room (15 min)
4. Site & Orientation (10 min)
5. Feel & Character (10 min)

**Question groups (renovation):**
0. Existing Conditions (varies — upload or guided survey)
1. What's Wrong (10 min)
2. What's Fixed (10 min)
3. What You Need (15 min)
4. How Far Will You Go (5 min)
5. Priorities & Feel (10 min)

**After each group:**
- Summary of what they entered
- Option to edit before moving on
- Preview of what this determines (optional)

**Completion screen:**
- "Thank you! Your design is being generated."
- "Your designer will review before your meeting."
- Confirmation of meeting date/time

### What Happens Behind the Scenes

1. **Answers stored** in project database
2. **System generates** initial plan from answers
3. **Designer notified** that questionnaire is complete
4. **Plan available** for designer review

## Phase 3: Designer Review

### Before Meeting with Client

**Review questionnaire answers:**
- Read through all responses
- Flag anything unclear or contradictory
- Note questions to ask in meeting

**Review generated plan:**
- Does it make sense given the answers?
- Any obvious issues the system missed?
- What are the key tradeoffs the system made?

**Prepare for meeting:**
- List of clarifying questions
- Points where client needs to make decisions
- Alternative options to discuss

### Common Pre-Meeting Questions

- "You said X in question 3 but Y in question 7 — which is more important?"
- "The system put the office here because of your answer about client visits — is that right?"
- "Your budget and your wishlist don't quite align — let's talk about priorities"
- "You didn't mention storage — is that not a concern, or did you forget?"

---

## Phase 4: Meeting 1 — Plan Review

### Meeting Structure (60-90 minutes)

**1. Check-in (5 min)**
- Thank them for completing questionnaire
- Confirm they're ready to see the plan

**2. Review their answers (10 min)**
- Quick summary of what they told you
- "Here's what I heard..."
- Chance to correct any misunderstandings

**3. Present the plan (20 min)**
- Walk through the generated design
- Explain how each part connects to their answers
- "You said you cook together, so the kitchen has an island facing the living room"
- "This bedroom is 12x12 because you said queen bed with walking space on both sides"

**4. Get reactions (20 min)**
- What feels right?
- What feels wrong?
- Any surprises?

**5. Identify changes (20 min)**
- Which answers need to change?
- Are there questions you didn't answer completely?
- Any new requirements that came up?

**6. Next steps (5 min)**
- You'll update the questionnaire answers
- System regenerates
- Review at Meeting 2 (or async if changes are minor)

### Key Phrases for the Meeting

**Connecting plan to answers:**
- "This is here because you said..."
- "The system prioritized X because you ranked it higher than Y"
- "This room is this size because you listed [furniture]"

**When something's wrong:**
- "Let's look at what you answered for that..."
- "If we change that answer to reflect what you actually want, the plan will update"
- "That's a different priority than you indicated — let's adjust"

**When there's a conflict:**
- "You want A and B, but they're in tension. Which matters more?"
- "The system had to choose — here's why it went this way"
- "If we change this, it affects that — is that okay?"

---

## Phase 5: Iteration

### Updating Answers

After Meeting 1:
1. Update questionnaire answers based on discussion
2. Note what changed and why
3. Regenerate plan

Keep a change log:
```
Version 2 — After Meeting 1 (2024-01-15)
- Changed: Bedroom 2 now needs to fit queen instead of full
- Changed: Office priority increased from "nice to have" to "required"
- Added: Forgot to mention need for dog washing station
- Changed: Ranked natural light higher than open plan
```

### Client Reviews Update

Options:
- **Async:** Send updated plan with notes, client reviews on their own
- **Quick call:** 15-min video call to walk through changes
- **Meeting 2:** Full meeting if significant changes

Most projects: async review is enough if changes were minor.

---

## Phase 6: Confirmation

### Design Sign-Off

When client is happy with the plan:

1. **Document final answers**
   - This is what you asked for
   - This is what the plan delivers
   - Signed/acknowledged by client

2. **Lock the design**
   - Changes after this point are change orders
   - New answers = new design = additional fee

3. **Move to next phase**
   - Documentation / construction drawings
   - Permitting
   - Pricing / bidding

### What Gets Signed

```
Design Confirmation — [Project Name]

The attached design is based on the following inputs:
- Questionnaire completed: [date]
- Revisions: [list versions and dates]
- Final answers: [link or attachment]

Key design decisions:
- [3 bedroom, 2 bath single story]
- [Open plan kitchen/living/dining]
- [Primary bedroom with ensuite and walk-in]
- [Home office with separate entrance]
- [Budget target: $X]

Client confirms:
- The design reflects their stated requirements
- Changes after this point may incur additional fees
- They have reviewed the questionnaire answers and they are accurate

Signed: _________________ Date: _______
```

## Handling Edge Cases

### Client Won't Complete Questionnaire

**"Can't we just meet and talk through it?"**

Response:
- "The questionnaire helps us make the most of our meeting time"
- "Instead of spending an hour on discovery, we spend it on your actual design"
- "I'll have something to show you instead of just taking notes"

If they refuse:
- Offer to do it together (screen share, you fill in as they talk)
- Takes longer, but captures the info
- Or: they're not the right client for this process

### Client Gives Incomplete Answers

**"I don't know" for too many questions**

System can:
- Flag minimum required answers
- Use defaults for truly optional items
- Generate plan with assumptions noted

Designer should:
- Review assumptions before meeting
- Discuss unknowns in meeting
- Document decisions made verbally

### Client Changes Mind After Sign-Off

**"Actually, we want 4 bedrooms now"**

This is a change order:
- Update questionnaire answers
- Regenerate plan
- Review impacts (size, cost, timeline)
- New fee if significant

The documented answers protect everyone:
- "Here's what you originally said"
- "Here's what you're changing"
- "Here's the impact"

### Multiple Decision-Makers

**Couples, families, business partners**

Options:
- Both complete questionnaire separately → compare answers → resolve conflicts
- Complete together (one form, discuss each question)
- One completes, other reviews and edits

Conflicts surface early:
- "You said open plan, your partner said defined rooms"
- "Resolve this before we design"

## Metrics to Track

### Process Health

- **Questionnaire completion rate:** What % of sent questionnaires get completed?
- **Time to complete:** How long does it take clients?
- **Questions skipped:** Which questions get left blank?
- **Revision cycles:** How many iterations to get to sign-off?

### Design Quality

- **Meeting count:** How many meetings to finalize?
- **Change orders:** How many changes after sign-off?
- **Client satisfaction:** Did the process feel efficient?

### Business Impact

- **Time saved:** Hours per project vs. traditional process
- **Proposal-to-project conversion:** Does showing a plan early help close?
- **Scope creep reduction:** Fewer undocumented assumptions

## The Pitch to Clients

**Traditional process:**
"Let's meet and talk about what you want. I'll take notes, go back to my office, and come up with some ideas. We'll meet again to see if I understood you correctly. Repeat until we converge."

**This process:**
"Fill out a questionnaire about how you live. When we meet, I'll already have a design based on exactly what you told me. We'll spend our time refining, not discovering."

**The difference:**
- Faster
- Nothing lost in translation
- Your words become the design
- Changes are traceable
- No "that's not what I meant"

## Summary

| Phase | Traditional | New Process |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Discovery | 1-2 hour meeting, notes | 30-60 min questionnaire, async |
| Initial design | Days of designer interpretation | Generated from answers |
| First review | "Here are some ideas" | "Here's what you asked for" |
| Iterations | 3-5 cycles typical | 1-2 cycles typical |
| Sign-off | Verbal, vague | Documented, traceable |

The questionnaire isn't paperwork. It's the design process — front-loaded, documented, and directly connected to the output.
